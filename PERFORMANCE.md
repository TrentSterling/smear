# SMEAR performance

V16 / 0.16.0 keeps approximately 60 FPS at 3000x1800 across idle, pistol, Spill, drag and Chaos in hardware Chrome and Firefox. Chrome CPU p99 is 1.6 to 1.8 ms, compute p99 is 1.18 to 3.74 ms and render p99 is 3.01 to 4.65 ms. Firefox CPU p99 is 1 to 2 ms, compute p99 is 1.17 to 3.18 ms and render p99 is 2.56 to 3.27 ms. The reported end-to-end tick rates are 119.1 to 119.6 in Chrome and 115.8 to 117.4 in Firefox, including harness inspection overhead; physics still advances at 1/120 second per tick. These are presentation-capped workloads, not uncapped throughput measurements.

The finite liquid field adds 20.25 MiB to the existing GPU storage and up to three compute dispatches per submitted frame. Pigment and skin resolutions, the 15-body rig, nine solver iterations, 900-drop capacity and 54 bristles remain unchanged. Both browsers pass gameplay, native profiling, nine wet-contact cases and eight film cases (68 checks total). Normal-play audits show no body or pigment downloads, canvas reads or WebGL contexts. In the finite-pool fixture, footprint area grows from 0.11 to 0.4325 square metres over eight seconds while mobile plus dried volume changes by less than 0.46%. A clean contact shifts the existing liquid centroid 5.82 cm with fresh deposition disabled. See [the V16 receipt](docs/qa/film-v16.json) for scope, measurements and matched MP4s.

The local V15 / 0.15.0 wet-contact pass holds approximately 60 FPS in all five native 3000x1800 Chrome workloads, with 119.0 to 119.5 simulation ticks/second. CPU p99 is 1.6 to 1.7 ms, compute p99 is 1.18 to 2.36 ms and render p99 is 2.75 to 4.00 ms. Dragging and Chaos have the highest compute p99, compared with 2.03 ms or less across the original V10 workloads. These measurements are presentation-capped. The contact snapshot adds 116.44 MiB of GPU storage; only binned paint tiles are copied per frame. Normal-play audits show no body-pose or pigment readbacks, no canvas reads and no WebGL context. The focused transport fixture preserves the total pigment alpha within 0.001% while moving existing colored pigment beyond its initial footprint, with fresh deposition disabled. See [the V15 receipt](docs/qa/wet-v15.json).

The local V14 / 0.14.0 dummy finish holds approximately 60 FPS in all five native 3000x1800 Chrome workloads. CPU p99 is 1.2 to 1.5 ms, compute p99 is 0.92 to 1.84 ms and render p99 is 2.69 to 3.15 ms. The finer surface costs more GPU time than V13 (render p99 1.77 to 2.36 ms), while staying within the measured 60 Hz frame budget. The shared skin is 173,174 vertices and 346,128 triangles plus its offset shell, with no open edges, inverted or degenerate triangles, or invalid skin weights. Final model inspection boot took 3.87 seconds. All six motion cases, all 75 receiver mappings, native paint and tool-input checks pass. See [the final V14 receipt](docs/qa/dummy-v14.json).

The prior V13 / 0.13.0 candidate booted in 3.73 seconds in the final fresh-profile Chrome startup inspection, compared with 6.34 seconds for the preserved V12 build. These measurements include CDP CPU sampling and are single-run observations; nearby iterations ranged from 3.6 to 4.0 seconds. SDF generation itself fell from 2.61 to 1.28 seconds. Independent pipeline compilation overlaps geometry generation, so phase times do not sum to total boot time. All 12 skin/shell attribute and index hashes match V12 byte for byte.

The final native 3000x1800 Chrome workloads stay around 60 FPS. CPU p99 is 1.3 to 1.6 ms, compute p99 is 0.92 to 1.84 ms, and render p99 is 1.77 to 2.36 ms. Chrome and Firefox gameplay pass 10 checks each; the native GPU suite passes seven. First-person input checks, the SDF topology/weight audit, and the art/mapping gauntlet also pass. See [the V13 receipt](docs/qa/presentation-v13.json).

The prior V12 / 0.12.0 motion candidate held approximately 60 FPS in all five native 3000x1800 Chrome workloads. CPU p99 is 1.4 to 1.6 ms; GPU compute p99 is 0.98 to 1.77 ms and render p99 is 2.10 to 2.56 ms. Actual simulation rate is 119.3 to 119.5 ticks/second. These are presentation-capped measurements, not uncapped maximum throughput. No body-pose or pigment reads occur during normal play. See [the V12 motion receipt](docs/qa/motion-v12.json).

The 24-second walking inspection measures opposite arm/leg correlation at -0.943, compared with -0.184 before the change. Six movement scenarios verify walking, floor-supported recovery, fallen heading and grab interruption. Maximum joint separation across the six scenarios is 4.94 cm. Upright posture is reached after 3.0 to 4.4 seconds in the uninterrupted recovery cases; the controller waits until its staged sequence finishes before returning to walking. The mesh and body-buffer sizes remain unchanged from 0.11.1; two reserved vectors hold the recovery snapshot.

The earlier V11 / 0.11.1 SDF dummy candidate held approximately 60 FPS in the five native 3000x1800 Chrome workloads. CPU p99 was 1.5 to 1.8 ms; GPU compute p99 was 0.98 to 1.90 ms and render p99 was 2.42 to 3.54 ms. Actual simulation rate was 119.3 to 119.7 ticks/second. The added face, chest and joint detail increased render cost from the earlier 0.11.0 range of 1.77 to 2.23 ms while remaining within the measured 60 Hz frame budget.

The shared dummy skin has 133,140 vertices and 266,060 triangles, plus its offset shell. All 15 GPU bodies participate in skinning; neck and waist have blended weights. Geometry is generated once at startup (6.00 seconds for complete hardware Chrome boot in the final inspection run). No body-pose reads, pigment reads, or CPU skinning uploads occur during normal play. Chrome and Firefox gameplay, the native Chrome profiles, 24-second locomotion/mapping checks, and the closed-mesh/weight audit passed. Six model views and reach, crouch and neck/waist flex poses were inspected. See [the 0.11.1 receipt](docs/qa/dummy-v11-1.json) and [the earlier V11 receipt](docs/qa/dummy-v11.json). Video recording is separate from profiling.

The local V10 / 0.10.0 art candidate holds approximately 60 FPS in the native 3000x1800 Chrome idle, pistol, Spill, drag and Chaos workloads. The presentation cadence is 60 Hz; these are not uncapped maximum FPS measurements. CPU p99 is 1.5 to 2.4 ms, GPU compute p99 is 0.98 to 2.03 ms, and render p99 is 0.92 to 1.18 ms. The same workloads held 60 FPS before the pass; Chaos compute p99 remains 2.03 ms. Audio runs during tests with browser output muted.

Uniform room paint density increases pigment storage from 96.58 to 116.44 MiB. Simulation remains 120 Hz with nine solver iterations, 900 droplets, 54-bristle strokes, 4x MSAA and a 2048 shadow map. Captures run separately from profiles. The art gauntlet also checks foot placement and all 75 receiver mappings; see [the V10 comparison receipt](docs/qa/art-v10.json).
